\begin{abstract}
Small open-weight language models are attractive for retrieval-augmented
generation (RAG) because they are deployable under tighter cost and
hardware budgets, but prior work shows that RAG systems still struggle
with faithfulness, negative rejection, and noisy evidence handling,
especially in small-to-mid model settings~\cite{mala2026faithfulness,chen2023rgb,zeng2025rare}.
Existing evaluations often emphasize answer quality while underreporting
latency, TTFT, and monetary cost, which makes deployability comparisons
incomplete~\cite{wu2024survey,brown2025systematic}. This paper presents
a fixed-retriever benchmark for small-model RAG on a HotpotQA distractor
subset, varying only generator choice and a corrective retrieval stage
while logging retrieval quality, adjusted accuracy, faithfulness,
abstention, latency, TTFT, and two cost axes from reproducible
per-query artifacts~\cite{mala2026faithfulness,es2023ragas,saadfalcon2023ares,elkiran2026empirical}.
Retrieval is held constant across all cells (BM25 sparse candidates
followed by a MiniLM cross-encoder reranker), and only the generator and
the presence of a corrective retrieval stage vary across a $3 \times 2$
grid of three small open models and two pipelines. Every cell is scored
on retrieval quality, adjusted accuracy, faithfulness, abstention,
end-to-end latency, time to first token, and two cost axes: an
API-equivalent cost and a GPU-amortised cost. We report results for
$n=300$ queries per cell on a HotpotQA distractor subset.
