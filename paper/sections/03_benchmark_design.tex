\section{Benchmark design}
\label{sec:design}

\textbf{Retrieval is fixed, and that is the point.}
The benchmark answers ``which small model should I pick under a fixed
retrieval policy?'', so retrieval must not vary between cells---otherwise
two cells can differ by more than their model, which is exactly the
confound that makes quality-only rankings misleading~\cite{rasheed2025empirical}.
Component studies show that retriever choice dominates much of the
variance in downstream quality, while rerankers can still shift
correctness~\cite{elkiran2026empirical}. Every cell therefore uses the
same fixed retriever:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item BM25 sparse candidate pool via \texttt{bm25s} (depth 30),
    \item MiniLM-L6 cross-encoder reranker over the 30 candidates,
          returning the top 10.
\end{enumerate}

There is no dense retrieval arm. On the full 483,696-paragraph HotpotQA
distractor corpus, the sparse pool already holds at least one gold
paragraph $98.00\%$ of the time, and the reranked top-10 reaches
$97.67\%$, so even a perfect dense fusion could add at most $0.33$
percentage points of recall@10---far too little to justify varying the
retriever between cells. The reranker runs in every cell and is not an
experimental axis. This design isolates \textbf{generator and
corrective-stage effects} under a constant retrieval policy; it is not a
claim about the globally best RAG architecture~\cite{elkiran2026empirical}.

\textbf{Matrix.} Three small open generator models $\times$ two pipelines
(gives 6 cells, Table~\ref{tab:matrix}): \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct},
\texttt{Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507}, and \texttt{LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct}
(Llama requires an accepted licence). The pipelines are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{baseline}: retrieved context is passed directly to the
          generator.
    \item \texttt{crag}: each retrieved context is scored by a local
          corrective evaluator (a \texttt{flan-t5-base} proxy for CRAG's
          trained 0.77B T5-large evaluator) and one of
          \texttt{Correct}/\texttt{Incorrect}/\texttt{Ambiguous} is
          triggered. On \texttt{Correct} and \texttt{Ambiguous}, context
          is refined with decompose-then-recompose strip filtering; on
          \texttt{Incorrect}, context is emptied and the model must
          abstain or answer from parametric memory---there is no live
          web-search fallback, so an \texttt{Incorrect} action is treated
          as a measured abstention rather than rescued accuracy. This is
          a derivative corrective design, not a full CRAG
          reproduction~\cite{yan2024crag}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Judge.} Faithfulness and relevance are scored by a separate judge
model (\texttt{Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3}, served as an AWQ checkpoint) on
its own vLLM endpoint, never concurrently with the generator. The server
identities of both judge and generator are recorded in every scorecard; a
gate enforces \emph{judge $\neq$ generator} so no cell can be
self-scored by the model that wrote its answer (Section~\ref{sec:method}).

\textbf{Data.} HotpotQA distractor subset, sampled once at $n=300$
queries with seed 42 from the train split that also builds the
corpus---90,447 rows yielding 483,696 paragraphs after title--text
deduplication. Eval and corpus come from the same split by design, so
every query's gold paragraphs are present in the index (this makes the
bench in-domain rather than out-of-domain; see Threat T3,
Section~\ref{sec:threats}). HotpotQA is a useful multi-hop benchmark but
is Wikipedia-centric and less reflective of proprietary or dynamic
production corpora~\cite{wu2024survey,mala2026faithfulness}; Section~\ref{sec:threats}
discusses this limitation.
